## What it is

Drop offhand references to future hangs *before* any explicit
invitation, so the meet feels assumed rather than negotiated. By the
time you actually pick a time and place, the question is "when",
not "if".

This is the message-level version of leaning forward. You're not
asking permission — you're casually rolling forward and seeing if
she rolls with it.

Examples:

- "you're going to have to show me the actually-good cafe in this
  city. the lo-fi places are killing me."
- "we'll have to settle the saturday-morning vs friday-night debate
  in person."
- "this is the kind of thing we'll argue about over drinks."
- "putting you on the calendar for the bookstore tour I'm clearly
  not qualified to lead."

## When it works

- She's reciprocating substantively (rapport phase or warm
  qualifying). Don't time-bridge from a cold thread.
- The bridge is a sentence at most. Bury it; don't headline it.
- The reference is *specific* (a venue, an activity, a debate to
  settle). Vague "we should hang" is weaker than "we'll settle this
  over drinks."
- It implies a meet without asking. The actual ask comes 1-2 turns
  later when temperature is right.

## When it fails

- Used too early — she hasn't warmed up, and "we'll" sounds
  presumptuous. Wait for one warm reciprocation first.
- Stacked with a direct ask in the same message — you're either
  bridging or asking, not both at once.
- Bridge references something she didn't say she likes. Imposing
  your idea of the meet (e.g. wine bar) when she's a coffee person
  reads tone-deaf.
