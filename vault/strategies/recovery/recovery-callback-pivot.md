## What it is

**Targets:** re-engagement (off-axis). Recovery is not on the
linear phase-progress chain. **Trigger condition:** thread has gone
quiet 48-120h after a turn where she volunteered something
specific that's still unresolved or pivotable. The card targets
re-engagement via a remembered detail and a fresh angle —
distinct from `rapport-callback`, which operates on a warm thread.
Here the conversation is broken, not warm.

**Mechanisms:** A15 Zeigarnik (closing an open loop) + A1
pre-suasive reset (the callback installs the frame fresh) + A11
self-perception via remembered specificity + I10 storytelling
embedded callback.

The move references something specific she said earlier and
pivots to a small new angle. The callback proves he remembered;
the pivot gives her a new way back in that doesn't require her
to re-engage with the old (now stale) thread. Importantly the
callback closes a Zeigarnik loop that was open from her side
too — she said something, it didn't go anywhere, and the
unfinished thread was sitting in the back of her attention.
The recovery message resolves that loop in a way that *she*
gets credit for (it was her detail, after all), which fires
A11 self-perception: she remembers being interesting; she
remembers him having paid attention to that interestingness;
the inference is internal and feels organic rather than
manipulated.

The mechanism stack: A15 Zeigarnik does the heaviest lifting —
unresolved narratives are remembered better than resolved ones,
and the callback retrieves an open loop *she* had been
unconsciously holding. Resolution-by-callback is satisfying in
a way that fresh material isn't, because the resolution
discharges latent attention. A1 pre-suasive reset fires because
the callback re-installs a frame ("we had a thread going on
this") that the silence had let drift; the callback restores
the substrate without needing to acknowledge the gap. A11
self-perception: she invested earlier (the volunteering of the
detail was investment); the recovery confirms the investment
mattered; she infers, to herself, that she must have been into
this thread because why else had she said that thing.

The pivot is the second half of the move — and the more
delicate. Pure callback ("you said you were trying that
new pottery class — how'd it go?") is technically valid but
risks landing as polite check-in (A16 autonomy violation
disguised as interest). The pivot adds a fresh angle that
gives her something *new* to react to: "you said you were
trying pottery — settled it for me, am I a wheel-thrower or
a hand-builder type?" The callback retrieves; the pivot
re-engages.

The art of insinuation (H7) operates here too — the callback
implies he's been carrying her thread without stating it. The
inference is hers; self-perception locks it in.

Example illustrations:

- "you said the bookstore on calle 5 was the move. went last
  weekend, you were right, now I owe you a different
  recommendation."
- "the running argument from before — settled it. I'm team
  late-dinner. you?"
- "saw the place you mentioned. it has a goat in the window.
  this changes everything."

Each one names her detail (callback, A15 closure), then pivots
to a new fork (re-engagement, A1 reset).

## When it works

- **The earlier detail was specific and felt important to her.**
  Generic callbacks ("you mentioned you like coffee") don't
  retrieve a real loop — there was no Zeigarnik tension on a
  detail she gave casually. The callback has to be on something
  she *invested* in saying. I5 IOI in the original message
  predicts whether the callback will retrieve.
- **The pivot creates a fresh question, not a re-litigation of
  where things went quiet.** The new angle has to *be* new — a
  fork, a reversal, a tangent — not a continuation of the dead
  branch.
- **48-120h gap.** Below 48h there's no Zeigarnik tension yet —
  the loop hasn't accumulated enough latent attention to be worth
  closing. Past ~5 days the detail has decayed and the callback
  reads as scrap-recovery; `recovery-clean-reopen` is better at
  that range.
- **Tone is light.** No making-a-case, no "I've been thinking
  about what you said about…" — that converts the callback from
  insinuation (H7) to declaration, which collapses the mechanism.
- **Real recall, not log-grepping.** If the callback reads like
  it was retrieved from notes rather than remembered, the
  authenticity-detector fires. The callback should land as
  *I happened to think of you because of this*, not *I went
  back through our thread to find a hook*.

## When it fails

- **Surveillance-y register → A10 sociometer leak + A14 otherness
  collapse.** Callback to a tiny detail across a long gap reads
  as effort-to-track, which inverts the mechanism (A11) — instead
  of "he heard me" it reads as "he was watching." The threshold
  is subtle but real; the heavier the recall and the longer the
  gap, the more it tips toward surveillance. Closer details, light
  touch.
- **Overplayed weight → A16 autonomy violation + sociometer leak.**
  "I've been thinking about what you said about…" frames the
  callback as significant *to him*, which forces her into
  evaluator-frame on his attention to her. The whole move's
  power is the lightness of insinuation; weight collapses it.
- **Pivot is the same kind of question that died last time → I5
  IOD reinforcement.** If the original loop died because polite
  questions stalled, a pivot to another polite question stalls
  the same way. The pivot has to introduce a new register, a
  new fork, or a take.
- **Callback without pivot → A16 autonomy violation as polite
  check-in.** "How'd the pottery class go?" is a callback with
  no fresh angle — reads as polite-friend energy, asks her to
  carry the next move alone, fails A11 because there's no new
  investment fork to step onto. Always callback + pivot.
- **Callback to a generic detail → A15 misfire.** No Zeigarnik
  tension to close, so the callback retrieves nothing. The
  detail has to have been a specific volunteer.
