## What it is

**Targets:** logistics 0 → 60+ via either-or framing. **Hard
prerequisite gate:** escalation ≥ 60 — the move presupposes mutual
interest and converts the question; without that substrate, it
reads as presumptuous rather than autonomy-supporting.

**Mechanisms:** A16 SDT autonomy (felt choice) + A17 Voss
calibrated questions + A3 commitment compression + A1 pre-suasive
frame.

The move offers two real options on day, place, or window:
"thursday or friday?" / "wine bar on smith or the noisy one, your
call." The frame conversion is the entire move — a yes-or-no
question on whether-to-meet becomes a which-of-these-meets
question, and *both options pre-commit her to meeting*. The
alternative she's choosing between is no longer "yes versus no"
but "option A versus option B."

The mechanism stack: A16 autonomy is the deepest lever in the
catalogue — felt choice activates engagement; felt grant-of-
permission destroys it. A binary close is autonomy-supporting
because she's choosing freely between two things; it's
autonomy-violating only if both options are bad. A17 calibrated
questions land here too — "thursday or friday, your call" is a
calibrated question that shifts cognitive load to her in a way
that *supports* her competence (A16) rather than draining it (the
way "what do you want to do" drains it). A3 commitment ladder
fires because either pick is a step onto a concrete rung; the
yes-rung is now implicit in the question itself. A1 pre-suasive
frame: by presenting the meet as already happening — only the
parameters undecided — the message anchors the meet as
foreground, not as the thing being argued for.

The structural trick is the frame inversion. Open-ended
scheduling ("when works for you?") puts the planning load on her
and presents the meet as conditional; binary close presents the
meet as decided and only the parameter as live. Voss's principle:
ask the question that points at the decision you want made.

Example illustrations:

- "thursday or friday?"
- "wine bar on smith or the noisy one, your call."
- "early evening or later, what's your week look like?"
- "drinks or coffee, dealer's choice."

Each one names the meet as the substrate and invites her to author
the texture.

## When it works

- **Escalation ≥ 60.** Forward-leaning interest is mutual, the
  thread is pointed at a meet. The move is conversion, not
  invention; without escalation substrate, you're trying to
  manufacture the meet through framing alone.
- **She's said yes in principle** but specifics aren't locked.
  Either-or close completes the rung she's already committed to
  in spirit.
- **Both options are real** for you. Bait-and-switch ("oh I forgot,
  thursday's actually out") burns the next ask and reads as I6 DLV
  — fake options pattern-match as game-playing the moment one
  option evaporates.
- **The two options are adjacent.** Same week, similar window,
  similar register. Wide-spread options ("thursday or three weeks
  from now") signal you don't actually have a constraint.
- **She reads as wanting agency.** Match has expressed taste
  preferences, made counter-proposals before, signalled she likes
  to weigh in. The two-option frame gives her exactly the
  co-authorship she wants without flooding her with full planning
  load.

## When it fails

- **Three or more options → cognitive load (A18) + autonomy
  paradox (A16).** "Thursday or friday or saturday or sunday?"
  collapses into open-ended scheduling, which collapses into
  paralysis. Two is the autonomy sweet spot; three is the
  overwhelm threshold. Pick two real options and hold.
- **Skipped escalation prerequisite → A3 ladder violation +
  reactance misfire (A9).** Two-option close before mutual
  forward-leaning interest reads as forcing a fork that doesn't
  exist yet. She hasn't agreed there's a meet — being asked to
  pick between two of them activates resistance.
- **One bait option → I6 DLV + frame collapse.** If "thursday or
  friday" is really "I want thursday and added friday for cover,"
  she'll feel the seams. The detection collapses A1 frame trust
  for the rest of the thread.
- **Both options at her end of town when you're across the city
  → A16 autonomy violation via false generosity.** Looks like
  choice; isn't. Real two-option close requires both options
  workable for both parties.
- **She's a planner-type wanting full proposal at once →
  autonomy mismatch.** Some matches read two-option as making
  them do work that should already be done. For these,
  `logistics-specific-place` is the better fit — same A3 rung,
  different A16 calibration.
- **Permission-framed wrapper → A16 violation.** "Would you
  prefer thursday or friday if that's okay?" reverts to grant-
  frame. The autonomy-positive read requires the question to
  *be* the decision, not a request for permission to ask one.
