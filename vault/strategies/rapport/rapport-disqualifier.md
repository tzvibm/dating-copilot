## What it is

Flip the audition. Imply that *she* has to clear your bar, not the
other way around. Done playfully, not judgey. Reads as confidence
because you're not auditioning for her.

The line: it's directed at a *category* (her archetype, an opinion,
a position she took), never at her body, looks, or anything she
didn't choose.

Examples:

- "you'd have to convince me you're not just another foodie with a
  spotify list."
- "fair warning, I'm tough on cafe recommendations. you're either
  bringing your A game or we're not friends."
- "I'll be honest, I have low expectations of anyone who claims
  they 'love to travel' on hinge. defend yourself."

## When it works

- Voice notes allow flirty / playful register.
- Rapport is already warm — she's reciprocating substantively.
- The "bar" you're setting is something she opted into (an
  opinion, a stated preference), not something innate to her.
- Tone is amused, not stern. Read it aloud — if it lands as
  playful challenge, ship it. If it sounds like a job interview,
  scrap it.

## When it fails

- Used too early, before she's warm. Reads as chip-on-shoulder.
- Targets her body, looks, or anything she didn't choose. That's
  not a disqualifier; that's a neg, and they don't work.
- Stacking with another challenge in the same message — too much
  challenge, no warmth.
- The "bar" is genuinely arbitrary or mean. "you'd have to be
  smarter than the last one" — comes off as dismissive.
