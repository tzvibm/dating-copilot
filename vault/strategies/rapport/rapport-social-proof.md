## What it is

Drop a real, offhand reference to your life — what you're doing,
who you're with, what you're up to — *as context for something
else*, not as a performance. "We're getting drinks at hideout
thursday" beats "I have lots of friends and I go out a lot." The
first is a fact in passing. The second is a billboard.

Implies "this is a life you might want in on" without saying it.
Builds attraction passively while the move keeps moving forward.

Examples:

- "we ended up at this rooftop bar last night, surprisingly the
  best mezcal I've had here. you been?"
- "saturday I'm doing this hike with a couple friends, then probably
  drinks. but next week's wide open."
- "just got back from bogotá. 3 days of debating which empanada
  spot is best with my college friends. I lost."

## When it works

- The reference is a real fact, not invented. Fake social proof
  reads loud.
- It's *background*, embedded in something else (a take, a question,
  an invite). Never the headline.
- One reference per message. Stacking ("then drinks then…") becomes
  performance.
- The detail is concrete — names of places, who you were with,
  what you ate. Specifics make it real.

## When it fails

- Used as the headline ("just so you know I'm pretty popular") —
  this is performance, not proof.
- Reference is vague ("always with friends") — generic = doesn't
  signal anything.
- Stacking 3+ social proof signals in a row reads as an audition.
- Used to manufacture jealousy ("got drinks with another match
  last night"). Different game; not what this card is for.
