## What it is

**Targets:** rapport 40 → 70 with secondary lift on escalation
substrate; installs an *otherness with implied options* texture
that rapport mechanisms compound against.

**Mechanisms:** A4 social proof (incidental, mate-choice copying)
+ I8 the phantom + A7 scarcity (real options, leaked) + A14
otherness / desire-distance + A5 authority by leak.

The move drops a real, offhand reference to his life — what he's
doing, who he's with, what's on his calendar — *as context for
something else*, never as a headline. "We're getting drinks at
hideout thursday" beats "I have lots of friends and I go out a
lot." The first is a fact in passing; the second is a billboard.
The reference is incidental to whatever the actual move on the
turn is — a take, a question, a callback — and the proof leaks
through that move's structure.

The mechanism is A4 in its only operationally durable form.
Mate-choice copying (Place et al.) shows women rate a man as more
attractive when he's photographed receiving positive attention from
another woman; the textual analogue is references that imply a
populated, social, in-motion life. The crucial constraint is *
incidental*. Explicit social proof inverts: "girls always tell me…"
reads as compensation for absent value. The mechanism only fires
when the proof is incidental to a story about something else —
because then her brain registers the social context as background
truth rather than performed claim. I8 the phantom names the same
move from the PUA tradition: implied-but-never-explicit other-options
register. Plans she's not part of, friends mentioned without
elaboration, schedule density. Never "I have other matches" (the
DLV form); always "we're doing thursday at the wine bar" with no
specification of who *we* is.

A7 real-options scarcity rides along: a populated calendar is the
single most leveraged scarcity signal because it's verifiable by
its incidentalness. A14 otherness compounds — she registers him as
a man with a gravitational center of his own, which is the
substrate desire actually requires. A5 authority by leak fires when
the social references incidentally encode competence (a work trip,
a domain interest, a project) without claim. Sociometer-wise
(A10) the move is sociometer-positive precisely *because* it's
not a social-proof claim; the gauge reads "this is what's true,
not what's being shown."

The card's flavour is *populated-life background texture*. Not a
move that lands on its own; a move that runs underneath the actual
turn move and lifts the substrate. Used continuously at low level,
it builds the otherness/scarcity bed everything else rests on.

Example illustrations:

- "we ended up at this rooftop bar last night, surprisingly the
  best mezcal I've had here. you been?"
- "saturday I'm doing this hike with a couple friends, then
  probably drinks. but next week's wide open."
- "just got back from bogotá. 3 days of debating which empanada
  spot is best with my college friends. I lost."
- "got pulled into a deal call thursday. wednesday's the actual
  open night."

Each names a real specific thing — a place, a count of people, a
who, a what — and serves the move that's actually the turn (a
question, a meet-anchor, a take), not its own headline.

## When it works

- The reference is *real*. Fake social proof reads loud — A4
  inverts immediately on detection, and the inversion is more
  costly than absent proof. The mechanism's entire credibility is
  in the externalness of the fact.
- The reference is *background* — embedded in another move (a
  take, a question, an invite, a logistics fork). The headline is
  the move; the proof leaks. Foregrounded social proof inverts to
  DLV (I6).
- One reference per message. Stacking two ("then drinks then
  brunch then…") tips into performance and the mechanism inverts.
- The detail is concrete: place names, counts, who-with, what.
  Specifics make it real; vagueness ("always with friends") reads
  as filler and signals nothing.
- The reference *serves the move*. "Saturday I'm doing this hike
  with friends, but next week's open" is social proof in service
  of an A7-scarcity logistics-anchor. Pure social proof for its
  own sake reads as filler.

## When it fails

- **Headline social proof → A4 inverts to compensation signal.**
  "Just so you know I'm pretty popular" / "I have a lot of
  friends" / "girls usually..." — the explicit form inverts the
  mechanism completely. The brain reads stated proof as evidence
  of absent underlying value.
- **Vague reference → A4 fails to fire + A10 leak.** "Always out
  with friends" carries no specific texture, so it codes as
  performance rather than fact. The mate-choice-copying mechanism
  needs concrete social context to register; vagueness fails the
  external-truth test.
- **Stacking 3+ proof signals in a row → A4 inversion via density.**
  Multiple incidental references in close succession stops being
  incidental and starts being a CV. Once the brain pattern-matches
  it as performance, the entire substrate inverts and the rapport
  damage is steep.
- **Manufactured-jealousy frame → different (failing) move + A10
  crater.** "Got drinks with another match last night" is not what
  this card is for. Triangulation games activate anxious
  attachment and read as low-frame; the mechanism here is otherness
  via populated life, not manufactured competition.
- **Used as a fake reason to delay → A7 collapse (manufactured
  scarcity).** "Can't thursday, busy with friends" when he isn't
  reads as theatre the moment she pattern-matches. Real-options
  scarcity requires real options; absent that, the move inverts
  to engineered-busyness, which is exactly what A7 fails on.
- **Reference contradicts logistics push → frame incoherence.**
  Heavy social proof + "I have nothing going on this week, when
  works for you?" reads as contradictory. The phantom register
  (I8) requires consistency turn-over-turn; one message claiming
  density and the next claiming wide availability collapses both.
