## What it is

Open with a specific reference to a photo that has a non-obvious
detail — not the obvious thing (her dog, the beach), the second-most-obvious
thing (the book on the side table, the city skyline behind her, the
tattoo half-visible on her arm).

Two valid forms:
1. **Specific question** about the detail — short, easy to answer in
   five seconds. ("the bookstore in your 3rd photo, where's that.")
   This is the data-backed top-converter; it works because of
   cognitive ease + implicit reciprocity (specificity creates a
   small social pull to respond).
2. **Take + opening** about the detail — declarative, plus an
   invitation for her to volley. ("that café in your 2nd photo is
   on my list. need a verdict.")

## When it works

- Photos have at least one specific detail you can name without
  ambiguity. Generic beach pics / model shots → use a different
  strategy.
- Your reference is a single concrete thing — not a paragraph of
  "I read your profile."
- She can answer in five seconds. If your phrasing forces her to
  think, you've lost the cognitive-ease advantage.
- Tone is curious / casual, not flattering.

## When it fails

- Photos are generic / model-shot / nothing to grab → use
  `opener-flirty-edge` or `opener-lead-with-take` instead.
- The "detail" is something embarrassing or sensitive (her body,
  weight, anything she didn't deliberately include).
- The reference is vague ("nice tattoo") — fails the specificity
  test, falls back to generic compliment territory.
- The opener turns into an interrogation ("what book is that, also
  what do you do, also where are you from") — stack of questions.
