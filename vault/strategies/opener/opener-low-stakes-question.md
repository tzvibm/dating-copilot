## What it is

**Targets:** opener 0 → 40-60 via cognitive-ease entry when no
stronger hook exists; the controlled-fallback opener.

**Mechanisms:** A2 reciprocity at minimum viable investment + A18
cognitive ease + A1 pre-suasive frame (sustained, not amplified) +
implicit A16 competence cue via question structure.

The move asks one specific, low-effort question framed around a
small *choice* — not biography ("what do you do") but a calibrated
fork that pulls a sliver of personality without demanding any real
energy. The question is structured so that the *premise* implies he
already has a take; she's not being interviewed, she's being invited
into a small game where his side of the answer is already loaded.

This is the opener you reach for when the profile gives nothing —
no callable photo detail, no flippable prompt, no clear register
hook for lead-with-take or flirty-edge. The mechanism is more
modest than the others. A2 reciprocity fires at minimum viable size:
a 5-second-answerable question is the smallest possible ask, which
keeps the gradient from inverting into obligation alarm. Cognitive
ease (A18) does most of the work — text rewards density, and a
single sharp fork with implied position is denser than it looks.
The pre-suasive frame (A1) installed is *interesting-stranger-with-
opinions* — not the high ceiling of flirty-edge or lead-with-take,
but a stable mid-ceiling that doesn't trip the small-talk-interview
collapse. The implied-take structure ("wine or coffee for a first
read on someone" presumes he has a position on first-reads) signals
A16 competence in passing without claim.

The card's flavour is *competent default*, not bold opener. Use it
when the upstream selection said *no stronger move applies* — and
when used, lean on the implied-take structure to keep the message
from collapsing into pure-question interview-frame.

Example illustrations:

- "where do you actually go when you want to read."
- "wine or coffee for a first read on someone."
- "what's the move on a sunday with no plans."
- "best meal in this city, no diplomatic answers."

Each names a small choice, implies a position on his side, reads as
casual, and stays under one short sentence.

## When it works

- Her profile genuinely offers no callback, prompt-flip, or register
  hook — the stronger A2 specific-noticing or A1 frame-installation
  moves can't fire because there's no surface.
- The question is structured as a fork ("X or Y", "where actually",
  "what's the move") rather than open-ended biography. Forks imply
  a take; biography questions don't.
- The premise carries an implied position — "no diplomatic answers"
  is sociometer-positive (A10) because it signals he has a take and
  expects her to as well.
- One question only. Stacking collapses the move into interrogation.

## When it fails

- **Question is biography-tier → A1 small-talk-interview collapse.**
  "What do you do" / "where are you from" / "how was your day"
  installs the lowest possible pre-suasive ceiling and downstream
  hyperpersonal compounding (A18) reads everything later in
  interview-frame. Unrecoverable from message one.
- **No implied position → A10 sociometer leak.** A pure question
  with no take signals he's filling his own message with her
  energy, which the gauge reads as low-MV. The fork structure or
  qualifier ("actually", "no diplomatic answers") is what keeps the
  move sociometer-neutral.
- **Stacked questions → cognitive load (A18) + A2 inversion.** Two
  questions in one message reads as interview and dilutes the move.
  The opener has one job; pick.
- **Question requires real thought → cognitive ease violation.** A
  question she has to draft a paragraph to answer mis-sizes the
  reciprocity ask (A2) and she defers the reply. Five-second answers
  only at the opener phase.
- **Used when a stronger move was available → opportunity cost,
  ceiling capped.** This card's pre-suasive ceiling (A1) is mid by
  construction. Reaching for it when callback or lead-with-take was
  available leaves opener-progress on the table.
