## What it is

**Targets:** opener 0 → 60+ via a confident characterization of her
vibe, delivered as a binary take or fork.

**Mechanisms:** A1 pre-suasive frame + A10 sociometer-positive
(he sees her clearly, no validation request) + I1 cocky-funny +
A15 open loop (she must close the binary).

The move names the pattern formed by her stated interests or profile
choices and delivers it back as a confident read — a binary or fork
she can confirm, deny, or elaborate on. It is NOT bio-quoting
(question about a specific interest) and NOT a take from his world
(that's `opener-lead-with-take`). The subject is a characterization
of *her vibe pattern*, delivered as a confident statement.

The mechanism stack: A1 pre-suasive frame fires because the message
encodes "I have a read on you" — a sociometer-positive signal (A10)
that implies confidence without seeking approval. The cocky-funny
register (I1) comes from the binary framing, which implies he has
clear models for people. A15 Zeigarnik loop fires because a binary
question with no answer is cognitively uncomfortable — she has to
close it. The move creates low-effort cognitive engagement (cognitive
ease: she can answer in one word or one sentence) while establishing
that he's observant and confident.

Example illustrations (for active-foodie-social profile archetype):

- "Beach bars, cafe hopping, active lifestyle. Strong opinions or
  zero preferences. Which is it."
- "Foodie, active lifestyle, self-care. Either you have a whole
  system or you're just very good at saying yes to things."
- "Dog walker, beach bars, often at the gym. You're a morning
  person or you're trying to become one. Hard to tell."

Each names 2-3 things she opted into, frames a binary read on what
that signals, and ends with a short declarative hook — not a
fawning question.

## When it works

- Her profile has 2-3 visible interest markers that form a
  recognizable pattern. The characterization needs material.
- The read is generous or neutral — teasing, not dismissive. Teases
  what she opted into; never appearance, weight, or anything she
  didn't choose.
- Profile register is light-to-moderate (active, foodie, social,
  adventurous). Doesn't land as well on earnest/heavy profiles
  where the binary feels reductive.
- Used as a reset on a dead thread or as a first message. If there's
  already substantive exchange, leads are better calibrated to
  what she's actually said (not her profile).

## When it fails

- **Profile is thin** — one photo, no interests listed. Nothing to
  read. The move requires observable pattern material.
- **Binary comes across as a test or judgment rather than curiosity.**
  Tone is everything; "strong opinions or zero preferences" teases.
  "You seem like the type who..." judges. The difference is in
  framing the binary as observation vs. evaluation.
- **Stacked with a question or follow-up** — one move only. Adding
  "what do you think?" or any second thought dilutes it.
- **Read is too obvious or flattering** — "you're fun or you're
  boring" reads as try-hard. The binary has to be genuinely
  ambiguous enough that she needs to answer.
- **Deployed after rapport is established** — once she's shared
  substantive things, a profile-level characterization reads as
  ignoring what she's already said. Use early or not at all.
