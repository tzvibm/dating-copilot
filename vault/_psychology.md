# Psychology Reference

Canonical grounding for every phase, playbook, and strategy in this
vault. Files cite mechanisms by handle (e.g. *operates via reactance —
see `_psychology.md#a9-reactance`*) rather than re-deriving the theory.

The orchestrator reads this when reasoning about edge cases, when
choosing between strategies that target similar surface moves but
different subconscious dynamics, and when classifying why a move
landed or fell flat.

This doc names mechanisms at the *operational* level. Failure modes
are framed as **what stops the mechanism from firing** — what makes
her withdraw, the frame collapse, or the move register as the
opposite of intended. This is a tactical reference; surface posture
is "what works and why," not "what's permitted."

---

## A. Mechanisms

### A1. Pre-suasive frame
**Source.** Cialdini, *Pre-Suasion* (2016); Tversky & Kahneman (1974)
on anchoring.

The opening message installs the interpretive frame that anchors how
every subsequent message is read. Her brain decides in seconds what
category this thread belongs to: interview, supplicant, peer-banter,
intriguing-stranger, sexual-undercurrent. The category sets a *ceiling*
on the warmth, wit, and intimacy the rest of the thread can reach.

**Application.** Choose openers that anchor the frame you want to
live in. "What do you do?" anchors interview-frame. "You're either a
planner or chaos, no in-between" anchors playful-judgment-frame with
you as judge. The first frame caps low; the second caps high.

**Failure mode.** Anchoring high then dropping (witty opener → boring
follow-ups) creates more disappointment than starting low — the
hyperpersonal effect (A18) compounds the drop. Sustainable frame >
spike-and-fade.

---

### A2. Reciprocity gradient
**Source.** Cialdini, *Influence* (1984); Regan (1971), Coke-and-
raffle-tickets study.

Humans feel discomfort accepting value without returning it. The
discomfort metabolizes as warmth or compliance toward the giver.
What matters is the **gradient**: investment slightly under hers
compounds; investment significantly above hers triggers obligation
alarm and she withdraws to escape it.

**Application.** Per-message investment slightly below hers. Specific
non-obvious noticing creates reciprocal pull at manageable size.
Calibrated vulnerability invites disclosure.

**Failure mode.** Long messages, premature compliments, multi-paragraph
replies to her one-liners. The gradient flips and the felt obligation
to return-at-that-level reads as pressure, not generosity. She
disengages because matching feels like work.

---

### A3. Commitment ladder
**Source.** Cialdini; Freedman & Fraser (1966) foot-in-the-door; Bem
(1972) self-perception.

Once she takes a small action consistent with interest, she infers —
to herself — that she must be interested, because why else would she
be doing it. Each micro-investment compounds. The ladder runs roughly:
substantive reply → asks a follow-up → uses your name or a callback →
voice note → phone call → meet.

**Application.** Each turn should request a slightly larger micro-
investment than the last. Calibrated questions (A16) and forks-with-
takes elicit investment without feeling like asks.

**Failure mode.** Skipping rungs (asking for the meet before any
real exchange) violates the gradient; the brain refuses moves that
don't match prior consistency. Conversely, asking for too many
trivial yeses in sequence reads as procedural and breaks immersion.

---

### A4. Social proof (incidental)
**Source.** Cialdini; Asch (1956); Place et al. (2010) mate-choice
copying.

Under uncertainty about value, people use others' behavior as
evidence. In mate-choice copying, women rate a man as more attractive
when he's photographed receiving positive attention from another
woman.

**Application.** Implicit, never stated. References to friends, plans,
an active social life leak through stories about other things. Frame:
*my life is already populated and good; you'd be joining something,
not rescuing something*.

**Failure mode.** Explicit social proof inverts. "Girls always tell
me…" reads as compensation for absent value. The mechanism only fires
when the proof is incidental to a story about something else.

---

### A5. Authority by leak
**Source.** Cialdini; Buss (1989) cross-cultural mate preferences;
Milgram (1963) deference dynamics.

Demonstrated competence in a domain she finds interesting is one of
the most reliable attraction inputs in the empirical mate-preference
literature, second only to humor and warmth. The mechanism requires
that competence be *shown, never told,* and *incidental, never
headlined*.

**Application.** "Flying to Tokyo Thursday for a deal" mentioned
while declining a Thursday meet operates as authority-via-scarcity-
via-competence in one move. The competence has to leak.

**Failure mode.** Naming the credential ("I'm a senior VP", "I went
to Harvard") triggers reactance (A9) and reads as compensation. The
brain pattern-matches headline-status-claims as low-status
performance.

---

### A6. Liking via selective expression
**Source.** Cialdini; Byrne (1971) similarity-attraction; Aron et al.
(1997) closeness-generation; Montoya & Insko (2008) reciprocal-liking
meta-analysis.

Reciprocal liking — the perception that *she likes me* — is among
the most consistent predictors of attraction. But the signal value
collapses with indiscrimination: liking expressed to anyone communicates
nothing about her.

**Application.** Express liking selectively and earned. "Ok that line
was actually funny, you've been upgraded" lands; "you seem cool"
doesn't. The selectivity is what makes it land; the earned-ness is
what makes it *feel* like discrimination.

**Failure mode.** Indiscriminate flattery ("you're amazing", "you're
beautiful") generates zero reciprocal-liking signal because she can
correctly infer he'd say it to anyone. Worse, it signals low
discrimination, which lowers his sociometer reading (A10).

### A7. Scarcity (real options)
**Source.** Cialdini; Brehm (1966); Worchel et al. (1975) cookies-
study.

Things less available are valued more; threats to access produce
reactance and increased desire. The crucial distinction: real
scarcity (you have a life, finite time, real other options) vs.
manufactured scarcity (deliberate delays, fake-busy theater).
Manufactured scarcity is detectable and brittle; real scarcity built
on a full life isn't.

**Application.** Showing up as a man with a full calendar is the
single most leveraged scarcity move. Ending threads cleanly, not
reorganizing your life around her schedule, having other plans the
language of which leaks naturally.

**Failure mode.** Endless availability ("whenever works", "I'm free
all week") collapses scarcity completely. Engineered delays read as
theater the moment she pattern-matches.

---

### A8. Unity / co-conspirator frame
**Source.** Cialdini, *Pre-Suasion* (2016) — added as 7th principle.

Shared-identity ("we") framing is a stronger compliance lever than
mere similarity. The pronoun shift from you/I to we, deployed lightly
and ironically, prefigures a relational identity before one exists,
and self-perception (A3) does the rest.

**Application.** Co-conspirator language: "we'd be the worst
influence on each other," "this is going to be a problem,"
"alright we're doing this." Light, playful, never declared.

**Failure mode.** Premature heavy-unity ("babe", "love", "my girl")
when no relationship exists collapses otherness (A14) and triggers
withdrawal. The unity frame works when both parties can plausibly
deny it; the moment it's stated, the move's done.

### A9. Reactance / takeaway
**Source.** Brehm (1966); Brehm & Brehm (1981); Driscoll et al. (1972)
Romeo-and-Juliet effect.

When a behavioral freedom is threatened, motivation to reassert it
rises. Takeaways, push-pull, mock-disqualifications, and playful
withdrawal install a freedom (your approval, your continued attention)
she now has slight motivation to claim. The forbidden-fruit lever.

**Application.** Disqualify *categories she opted into* — opinions,
self-described archetypes, stated preferences — never traits or
appearance. "You'd have to convince me you're not just another
spotify-list foodie" works because the standard is game-able.

**Failure mode.** Lands on a real insecurity → injury, not arousal.
Brain doesn't misattribute pain as attraction; pain just registers
as a reason to leave. Premature deployment also fails: reactance
needs a freedom she's *already* mildly invested in, so it requires
warm rapport substrate.

### A10. Sociometer
**Source.** Leary & Baumeister (2000); Leary (2005).

Self-esteem is not a goal-state but a continuously-updated **gauge**
of one's social/mate value. Crucially, the gauge reads behavior
diagnostically: a man asking *"are you mad?"* is signaling that *his*
sociometer reads low, which is downstream evidence to *her* sociometer
that his actual mate value is low — high-MV men don't seek
confirmation.

**Application.** Treat any move whose function is to harvest
reassurance as a sociometer leak. Statements of interest are fine;
requests to confirm interest are not. Signal mate-value through
incidence (full life, options, frame stability), not declaration.

**Failure mode.** Every reassurance-seeking move ("did I say something
wrong?", "you seemed off", "still on for tonight??") drops her read
on his gauge. The asymmetry is steep — one anxious-coded message can
undo three good ones.

### A11. Self-perception / Ben Franklin effect
**Source.** Bem (1967, 1972); Jecker & Landy (1969).

People infer their attitudes from their own behavior. If she invests
in him — asks a follow-up, sends a voice note, makes a small
concession — she infers, *to herself*, that she must be interested,
because why else. The inference is internal and feels organic, not
manipulated.

**Application.** Every elicited micro-investment shifts her felt
interest upward by self-perception. Calibrated questions, forks, the
unity frame, mock-asks — all elicit investment without feeling like
asks.

**Failure mode.** Investment that feels coerced or performative
("just say yes!") gets attributed externally and the mechanism
doesn't fire — she labels her action as "going along," not "interested."

---

### A12. Misattribution of arousal
**Source.** Dutton & Aron (1974) Capilano Bridge study; Schachter &
Singer (1962); White et al. (1981) replication.

Physiological arousal is undifferentiated; the cortex labels it
post-hoc using contextual cues. Tension generates arousal that the
brain often labels as **attraction** if the proximate cause is the
attraction target. This is why agreeable, low-friction conversations
feel "nice" but generate no pull, and why playful conflict, mock-
challenges, and surprise turns generate the pull that gets attributed
to "I feel something around this guy."

**Application.** Tension with embedded warmth — teasing, pushing
back, mock-disqualifying, surprise pivots, pattern interrupts. The
arousal needs a positive context to be labeled as attraction rather
than annoyance.

**Failure mode.** Tension without warmth registers as hostility, not
attraction. Tension on a topic she's actually sensitive about
registers as cruelty. The original Dutton & Aron effect required the
target to be at least baseline-attractive — for text, the equivalent
is sufficient prior warmth in the thread.

### A13. Variable-ratio reinforcement
**Source.** Skinner (1953); Ferster & Skinner (1957).

Behavior reinforced on an unpredictable schedule is the most resistant
to extinction — the slot-machine effect. Predictable rewards extinguish
exploratory behavior; intermittent unpredictable rewards lock it in.

**Application.** Conversational rhythm naturally varies because life
varies — replies aren't instant, length isn't constant, warmth has
texture. The variation that comes from a real life produces the
variable schedule organically and locks in attention more reliably
than uniform high-engagement.

**Failure mode.** Two opposite failures. (1) Always-instant replies
flatten the schedule and her engagement habituates downward — there's
no anticipation to trigger return-checking. (2) Engineered delays
(timing replies to a stopwatch) get pattern-matched and read as
manipulation, which collapses trust faster than uniform availability
collapses pull. Natural variation only.

### A14. Otherness / desire-distance
**Source.** Perel, *Mating in Captivity* (2006).

Desire requires distance and the perception of the other as a
separate, somewhat-mysterious agent with her own gravitational
center. Intimacy collapses distance. The two are in productive
tension; over-prioritizing closeness extinguishes desire.

**Application.** Early dating's job is to oscillate, not monotonically
increase closeness. Be doing things she's not part of. Be the
protagonist of your own story, not a chorus to hers. Have a life
she's curious about because she can't see all of it.

**Failure mode.** Multi-paragraph emotional disclosure on day two
collapses the desire-gap. She enjoys it in the moment and goes cold
within 48 hours because the imaginative space that generated pull
has been closed. Daily good-morning texts produce the same collapse
slower.

### A15. Zeigarnik / open loops
**Source.** Zeigarnik (1927); Ovsiankina (1928).

Interrupted tasks and unresolved narratives are remembered better
than completed ones; the mind allocates ongoing attention to
unresolved content. Open loops attract cognitive return.

**Application.** Future-pacing ("when we go to that bar I have a
story for you"), cliffhangers ("remind me to tell you about the
goat"), strategic ambiguity. Ambiguity in a strong thread reads warm
because her imagination fills the gap (see A18 hyperpersonal).

**Failure mode.** Loops you never close feel evasive and erode
trust. Too many simultaneous loops feel manipulative. Each loop
needs a real promise of payoff or it stops working.

---

### A16. Self-Determination Theory: autonomy, competence, relatedness
**Source.** Deci & Ryan (1985, 2000); Ryan & Deci (2017).

Three universal psychological needs drive engagement. When an
interaction supports them, the person feels energized and self-
aligned; when it thwarts them, they disengage. **This is the deepest
mechanism on this list — most other moves are special cases of it.**

- **Autonomy.** Her felt sense that she's choosing freely, not being
  asked to grant. "You'd have to convince me you can hang" supports
  autonomy (she chooses to clear the bar). "Do you like me?"
  destroys it (she's forced into evaluator-frame).
- **Competence.** Banter is competence-display by both sides.
  Calibrated questions and labels make her feel sharp. Forks-with-
  takes give her something to be smart about.
- **Relatedness.** Real curiosity, real similarity, the unity frame
  (A8), shared humor cadence.

**Application.** Audit every move against the three needs. A move
that supports all three is approximately unstoppable. A move that
violates autonomy is the most reliable killer in the catalog.

**Failure mode.** "Can I take you out?" frames consent as binary
grant/deny — autonomy-violating. "Drinks Thursday — wine bar on
Smith or the noisy one, your call" frames her as co-author —
autonomy-supporting. Same intent, different mechanism activation.

### A17. Calibrated questions, labels, "no" as safety
**Source.** Voss, *Never Split the Difference* (2016); Lieberman et
al. (2007) on affect-labeling fMRI work.

- **Labels** ("it sounds like Tuesdays are brutal") name her
  emotional state, which validates and partially defuses it. fMRI
  work shows amygdala dampening on labeling.
- **Mirrors** (echoing her last 1–3 words) elicit elaboration with
  minimal pressure.
- **Calibrated questions** ("what would make this actually fun?")
  shift cognitive load while preserving autonomy.
- **"No" as safety.** Letting her say no without consequence makes
  her feel in control, which paradoxically increases yes-rate.
  "Totally fine if Thursday's a no" beats "you should come."

**Application.** Logistics and recovery especially. Labels diffuse
defensiveness. Calibrated questions get her elaborating without
feeling interrogated. Inviting "no" removes pressure and increases
yes.

**Failure mode.** Mechanical deployment reads as therapy-speak and
breaks the frame. Overuse of calibrated questions reads as evading
having a position.

### A18. Hyperpersonal effect (text-only)
**Source.** Walther (1996); Kruger et al. (2005).

Computer-mediated communication produces *intensified* impressions in
either direction. Text strips paralinguistic cues and replaces them
with timing, length, punctuation, and word choice as the entire
signal vocabulary. Receivers fill ambiguity with imagination — which
is high-variance and *direction-dependent on prior frame*.

Three operational consequences:

1. **Frame compounds asymmetrically.** Strong opener → ambiguous
   later messages read warm. Weak opener → same ambiguity reads cold.
2. **Latency is content.** Reply timing carries as much information
   as words. Unavoidable.
3. **Compression forces signal density.** Length should match signal.
   The single most underrated text move is the short, well-chosen
   line.

**Failure mode.** Treating text as transcript-of-speech misses the
medium. Long messages dilute. Sub-tonal cues (sarcasm, tease, warmth)
don't survive without scaffolding (the right punctuation, the right
register-shift).

---

### A19. Mate-preference asymmetries
**Source.** Buss (1989) 37-culture study; Buss & Schmitt (1993)
Sexual Strategies Theory; Fisher (2004).

Cross-culturally and at population level, women rate cues related to
status, resources, ambition, agentic competence, and mild dominance
(frame-stability, not aggression) somewhat higher than men do; men
rate cues related to physical attractiveness and youth somewhat
higher than women do. Both sexes rate humor and intelligence at the
top. **Effect sizes are real but moderate; within-sex variation
exceeds between-sex variation on most traits.**

**Application.** For men texting women: cues that incidentally signal
agency, having one's life together, frame-stability under pressure,
and humor (intelligence-signaling) reliably outperform their absence.
*Incidentally* is the operative word — these traits collapse the
moment they're claimed (see A5 authority-by-leak).

**Failure mode.** Over-indexing into caricature (alpha-larping)
trips authenticity-detectors. Under-indexing into pure deference
(mirror-only, no frame) reads as low-agency and triggers loss-of-
interest. The middle path is *quiet* signal.

## C. Goal-type weighting

Different timelines re-weight which mechanisms dominate. Same toolkit,
different dial settings.

### quick-meet-window
**Dominant.** Scarcity (A7) + commitment momentum (A3) + pre-suasion
(A1).

The opener does extra work because there's less recovery time. The
logistics ask comes earlier and more directly. The frame "if this
happens it has to be soon" must be installed early and credibly via
real scarcity (you're traveling, she's traveling).

**What changes vs. default.** Less rapport investment. More frame-
confidence. Compress phases — qualifying often skipped or folded
into rapport.

**What kills it.** Ambiguity. "Maybe sometime this week" doesn't fit
the timeline — signals you don't actually have a constraint, which
makes urgency feel manufactured.

### flexible-short-window
**Dominant.** Liking (A6) + commitment ladder (A3) + light Zeigarnik
(A15).

Default mode. Standard rapport-into-logistics arc with normal pacing.
Build real micro-rapport (3–10 quality exchanges), one or two open
loops, specific logistics once interest is mutual.

**What kills it.** Letting it drift past 2 weeks. Match-decay is
real — the longer between match and meet, the lower the meet
probability. After ~10 days the thread is going cold regardless of
how it feels.

### extended-window
**Dominant.** Zeigarnik (A15) + variable-ratio reinforcement (A13) +
otherness (A14).

Without the meet as natural close, the thread sustains its own
tension. Voice notes, calls, photos-from-your-actual-life do work
text alone can't. Open loops compound — "when we finally do this in
person" becomes a recurring frame.

**What kills it.** Daily good-morning texts. The relationship
pretending to be in a phase it's not. Closing the desire-gap before
proximity. No concrete plan-anchor on the horizon → thread has no
telos.

### decline-gracefully
**Dominant.** Autonomy (A16) + sociometer (A10).

All persuasion off the table. Acknowledge cleanly, don't argue.
"All good — appreciated the honesty, take care." Sociometer-positive
disengagement: she sees he can be told no without flinching, which
is the only opening that stays open if anything later changes.

**What kills it.** Trying to reverse the decline. "But what if we
just…". Sulky one-word replies. Re-pitching.

### reconnect-later
**Dominant.** Zeigarnik (A15) + scarcity-as-time (A7) + pre-suasion
reset (A1).

The reopener is a fresh first impression. Don't reference the old
thread's stuckness. Don't apologize for being slow. Anchor on
something present-tense ("just walked past that bar you mentioned,
can confirm it's still hostile to humans").

**What kills it.** "Hey stranger." "Long time no talk." "Sorry I
disappeared." All sociometer leaks — put her in evaluator-frame.

### just-being-polite
**Dominant.** Variable-ratio (A13) at low frequency.

Low-investment maintenance. Reply rate roughly matches hers. No
escalation moves. Holding pattern.

### platonic-curiosity
Out of scope for tactical optimization. Drop pursuit moves.

## D. Failure-mode catalog (mechanism-keyed)

The most common ways text moves backfire. Each named with the
underlying mechanism that misfired.

1. **Sociometer leak (A10).** Explicitly qualifying his own interest,
   asking for reassurance, apologizing for previous messages.
   Counter: let interest be inferred from chosen action.

2. **Anxious-attachment trigger (Bowlby).** Double-text after silence
   that references the silence. Signals anxious activation; on her
   end activates avoidant withdrawal or anxious mirroring.
   Counter: send nothing, or send something fully untethered to the
   prior silence.

3. **Autonomy violation (A16).** "Can I take you out?" / permission-
   framed asks. Counter: propose specific plan with options, easy out.

4. **Reciprocity inversion (A2).** Long heartfelt messages early
   collapse the gradient. Counter: investment slightly under hers
   until rapport established.

5. **Reactance miscalibration (A9).** Disqualifier landing on a real
   insecurity → injury, not arousal. Counter: target opinions and
   self-categories, never bodies or innate traits.

6. **Frame collapse via headline status (A5).** Naming the credential
   inverts authority. Counter: status leaks through context, never
   headline.

7. **Otherness collapse (A14).** Multi-paragraph emotional disclosure
   pre-meet closes the desire gap. Counter: meter disclosure to the
   rapport rung.

8. **Dead-air thread (A15).** Statement-statement-statement, no hook,
   no future-pace. She has nothing to do with the message.
   Counter: most messages should leave one open thread for her to
   pull on.

9. **Pattern-matched intermittency (A13).** Calculated delays read
   as game-playing. Counter: reply when natural; let life create
   variance.

10. **Logistics ambiguity (A3 + A16).** "We should hang sometime"
    puts planning burden entirely on her, no specific step offered.
    Counter: specific day, place, time, easy out.

11. **Over-confirmation on day-of (A10).** Multiple check-ins.
    Counter: one short warm confirm 2–4 hours before, then nothing.

12. **Ghost-protest.** She slow-replied; he goes cold-and-punishing.
    Reads as avoidant-coercive — worst attachment-system signal.
    Counter: meet her at the temperature she's at, no scorekeeping.

13. **Bio-quoting (A16 + A2).** Reading her bio back at her puts her
    in performance-frame and signals he made her profile the subject.
    Counter: bio calibrates register, never becomes topic.

14. **Stacked-question interrogation (A2 + A18).** Multiple questions
    in one message read as interview and dilute the move.
    Counter: one move per message.

15. **Spike-and-fade (A1 + A18).** High-frame opener, low-energy
    follow-ups. Drop is more disappointing than starting low.
    Counter: sustainable frame > optimized opener.

## E. Text-only courtship dynamics

Text-only adds three constraints on top of in-person dynamics:

**Latency is signal.** Reply timing carries information equal to or
greater than words. This is unavoidable. The rule is *natural variance
from a full life*, not engineered cadence.

**Imagination fills gaps direction-dependently (A18).** Strong prior
frame → her imagination reads ambiguous later messages warm. Weak
prior frame → same ambiguity reads cold. This is why early-thread
quality compounds disproportionately.

**Compression forces precision.** Speech tolerates filler;
text doesn't. Length should match signal. The most underrated text
move is the short, well-chosen line.

**Channel-jumps escalate sharply.** Voice note > text. Phone call >
voice note. Video > phone. Each upgrade is a commitment-ladder rung
worth several text-rungs. Use them deliberately to compress phase
progression.

**Screenshots are always live.** Anything sent can be group-chat'd.
This is a constraint on what to send (anything you'd be embarrassed
to have screenshotted is a strategy-design failure), not a tactical
lever.

## F. Greene — seducer archetypes (adoptable frames)

**Source.** Greene, *The Art of Seduction* (2001).

Greene catalogues nine seducer types — frames a man can adopt for a
match, calibrated to her archetype and the thread's temperature.
These are *registers*, not personas to wholesale impersonate. The
user's voice (`self/voice.md`) is the substrate; the seducer type is
the angle of attack within that voice.

For each type: the essence, the frame it installs, the mechanism it
leverages, and when to deploy.

### F1. The Rake
**Essence.** Hungry, irreverent, makes her feel she awakens something
in him. Compliments her in surgical, surprising ways.

**Frame.** *I am not safe and not sorry about it.* Pleasure-coded,
slightly transgressive, pushes against propriety just enough to feel
forbidden.

**Mechanisms.** Misattribution of arousal (A12) via transgression,
reactance (A9) when she pushes back, scarcity (A7) implied by
appetite for life.

**When to deploy.** Match has erotic register and is reciprocating.
Voice notes permit flirty/edgy. Avoid in cold openers (no warrant
yet).

### F2. The Ideal Lover
**Essence.** Reads what she lacks and provides exactly that — the
adventure she's craving, the intellectual sparring, the protector
she's missing. Tailored attention.

**Frame.** *I see what you actually want, and I'm bringing it to
you.* High-attunement, almost therapeutic in its precision.

**Mechanisms.** Liking via selective expression (A6), self-perception
(A11) — she sees herself as the kind of woman who's understood,
which becomes self-fulfilling.

**When to deploy.** When her bio/messages reveal a clear unmet need
(creative restlessness, intellectual hunger, escape-from-routine
energy). Don't deploy on a cipher — Ideal Lover requires real read.

### F3. The Dandy
**Essence.** Ambiguous, gender-bending, refuses to be pinned down.
Curiosity engine. Doesn't fit the mold she has for men.

**Frame.** *I am uncategorisable, and you'll spend energy trying to
categorise me.* Cognitive open-loop (Zeigarnik) embodied.

**Mechanisms.** Zeigarnik (A15) — she keeps trying to resolve the
ambiguity. Otherness (A14) — refuses the gendered scripts.

**When to deploy.** Sophisticated/jaded match who's bored of standard
masculine performance. Less effective with archetypes seeking
clarity.

### F4. The Natural
**Essence.** Childlike spontaneity, plays without self-consciousness.
Disarms by refusing to seem to be performing.

**Frame.** *I'm not trying. I'm just being. Come play.*

**Mechanisms.** Reciprocity (A2) — she relaxes guard because nothing
is being extracted. Otherness via non-performance (A14).

**When to deploy.** When she's been performing or guarded; gives her
permission to drop the mask. Never via canned material — the moment
it's optimised, the frame breaks.

### F5. The Coquette
**Essence.** Hot-cold, attainable-then-distant, intermittent rewards.
Trains her engagement on a variable schedule.

**Frame.** *You almost have me. You almost don't.* The pendulum is
the move.

**Mechanisms.** Variable-ratio reinforcement (A13), reactance via
withdrawal (A9), Zeigarnik (A15). Among the most operationally
powerful Greene types — and the easiest to overdo.

**When to deploy.** Mid-thread, after warmth is established. Pull
back exactly when she leans in. Not in opener (no warmth substrate).
Stop the moment it tips into anxious-attachment-induction — natural
withdrawal from a full life, not engineered absence.

### F6. The Charmer
**Essence.** Makes her feel like the centre of attention without
overt seduction. Listens, deflects from himself, returns focus to
her in flattering precision.

**Frame.** *Everything about you interests me, and nothing about me
needs your attention right now.* Sociometer-positive: he's not
seeking validation.

**Mechanisms.** Self-perception (A11) — she invests in answering
because his attention is drawing her out. Liking (A6). Reciprocity
(A2) gradient kept in her favour.

**When to deploy.** Rapport-heavy phases, especially with verbal/
narrative archetypes who want to be heard. Risk: tips into
interviewer-frame if no take of his own ever surfaces.

### F7. The Charismatic
**Essence.** Conviction radiating outward — about ideas, taste,
plans, life. The vision is contagious.

**Frame.** *I'm going somewhere; come with me.*

**Mechanisms.** Authority by leak (A5), pre-suasive frame (A1),
mate-preference cues for status/agency (A19). Powerful for high-
ambition archetypes.

**When to deploy.** When the user has real conviction to draw on —
this fails on canned charisma. The match responds to vision.

### F8. The Star
**Essence.** Ethereal, slightly distant, lets her project onto him.
The screen on which she paints her ideal.

**Frame.** *I am partly absent, partly luminous. Fill in the rest.*

**Mechanisms.** Otherness (A14), Zeigarnik (A15), hyperpersonal
imagination-filling (A18) — text amplifies this register.

**When to deploy.** Extended-window goals where mystery sustains
tension. Not for quick-meet — Star evaporates on contact, needs the
gap.

### F9. The Siren (gender-flipped: the Sensualist)
**Essence.** Pure pleasure-coded presence. Slows time. Sensual
attention.

**Frame.** *Time bends around me. Come into the slowness.*

**Mechanisms.** Misattribution (A12) via embodied register,
otherness (A14).

**When to deploy.** Late-rapport / escalation with a match who's
warmed substantively. Not for openers; not for short-attention
threads.

## G. Greene — seduction arc (mapped to phase model)

Greene's 24-step arc compresses into four meta-phases. Each maps onto
our `phase_progress` axes:

| Greene phase | Our phases | Dominant move |
|---|---|---|
| **Stir interest and desire** | opener, early rapport (0-30) | Mixed signals, an aura that's both available and elusive. Make first contact indirect — through context, reputation, or angle, not direct request. |
| **Create pleasure and confusion** | rapport (30-70), early qualifying | Effect mild disorientation. Switch tones. Pleasure-coded moments alternated with absences. The thread becomes affectively rich and somewhat unpredictable. |
| **The deepening effect** | qualifying (60+), escalation | Stir up the transgressive. Mix pleasure with mild pain (tease, mock-disappointment, light withdrawal). She becomes invested in resolving the tension. |
| **Moving in for the kill** | escalation (60+), logistics | Drop the ambiguity. The invitation is direct, specific, frame-confident. Greene's principle: at the close, the indirect approach inverts — clarity becomes the seduction. |

**Operational note.** The arc is not strictly linear. Late-arc moves
can be re-injected mid-rapport to reset tension; early-arc indirection
should not appear at logistics. Use the table as gating, not script.

## H. Greene — tactical principles (signature moves)

### H1. Send mixed signals
Project two contradictory things at once: warmth + slight distance,
attention + absence, agreement + tease. The contradiction is the
hook — her mind keeps trying to resolve which signal is "real."

**Mechanisms.** Zeigarnik (A15) + variable-ratio (A13) +
hyperpersonal ambiguity (A18).

### H2. Calculated absence
The withdrawal that makes presence felt. Brief, real, never
explained. She notices the silence, feels its weight, then your
return lands at higher temperature than the previous turn.

**Mechanisms.** Reactance (A9), scarcity (A7).

**Failure mode.** Engineered absence pattern-matches as game-playing.
The absence has to feel natural — coming from a real life with
other commitments — or it inverts.

### H3. Stir up the transgressive
Introduce mild taboo. The forbidden register — not crassness, but
edge: the slightly inappropriate observation, the off-limits topic
broached lightly, the implication of something she shouldn't want.

**Mechanisms.** Misattribution of arousal (A12), reactance (A9).

**Failure mode.** Over-played, becomes vulgar. The art is the
*implication* of transgression, not the transgression itself.

### H4. Mix pleasure with pain
Compliment + tease. Warmth + mock-disappointment. The texture is
what creates pull — pure pleasure habituates fast, pure pain
extinguishes engagement, but the alternation locks attention.

**Mechanisms.** Variable-ratio (A13), misattribution (A12).

### H5. Create temptation, then leave them wanting
Show enough to be tantalising, never enough to satisfy. The thread
that closes one loop while opening another. The voice note that
ends mid-thought. The story whose ending she has to ask for.

**Mechanisms.** Zeigarnik (A15), otherness (A14).

### H6. Enter their spirit (calibrated mirroring)
Adopt her register, vocabulary, cadence — then nudge it toward
yours. Greene's principle: meet them where they are before leading
them where you want them to be.

**Mechanisms.** Liking via similarity (A6), reciprocity (A2).
Compatible with Voss labels/mirrors (A17).

### H7. The art of insinuation
Suggest without stating. Imply meeting before asking. Imply intimacy
before claiming it. The reader does the work of inferring — and
that inference is hers, so it sticks (self-perception, A11).

**Mechanisms.** Self-perception (A11), Zeigarnik (A15), pre-suasion
(A1).

## I. PUA-tradition tactical framework

**Source.** Strauss, *The Game* (2005); David DeAngelo, Mystery,
Ross Jeffries, Tyler Durden, Real Social Dynamics-era community
synthesis. Operationally observant, often crude in originating
register. Filtered for text-only courtship; in-person-only tactics
(kino escalation, last-minute resistance, three-second rule literal)
omitted.

### I1. Cocky-funny (DeAngelo)
**Definition.** Statements that combine arrogant claim with
self-aware humour, so the arrogance reads as play. "You'd be lucky
to keep up with me on a tuesday" — the claim is bold; the wink
makes it land.

**Mechanism.** Simultaneously activates authority by leak (A5),
reactance (A9 light), and humour-as-competence-signal (A19). The
self-awareness prevents the authority claim from collapsing into
performance.

**Application.** Default register for openers and rapport in this
system, calibrated by `voice.md`. The single most reliable PUA-
tradition tactic that survives translation to text.

### I2. Push-pull
**Definition.** Statement that pulls her in (compliment, agreement,
warmth) immediately followed by a push (tease, withdrawal, mock-
disqualification), or vice versa. "ok that was actually clever, I'm
revising my opinion of you upward. don't let it go to your head."

**Mechanism.** Misattribution (A12) on the push, reciprocity (A2)
on the pull, variable-ratio (A13) at the message level.

**Application.** Mid-rapport through escalation. One push-pull per
turn maximum — stacking dilutes.

### I3. The Neg
**Definition.** Backhanded observation that registers as mild
critique without being insult. "you're trouble, I should know
better" / "weirdly, the spotify-playlist-girls always end up being
the most chaotic." Mystery's original framing was destabilisation;
the durable version is *playful frame-establishment that signals
non-supplication*.

**Mechanism.** Reactance (A9), sociometer signal (A10) — registers
that he's not auditioning for her approval.

**Application.** Rapport with warm matches who can carry it. Targets
*chosen categories* (her self-described type, her stated takes),
never her body or innate traits — the latter crosses from playful
critique into actual injury and the brain doesn't misattribute pain
as attraction. See A9 failure mode.

### I4. AMOG handling (Alpha Male of the Group)
**Definition.** Originally: handling competitive male presence in
nightlife. Text translation: handling shit-tests, third-party
mentions, ex-mentions, comparison frames ("my last guy was like
that…", "you sound just like every other guy on hinge").

**Mechanism.** Frame control. Refuse to enter the frame she's
inviting (defensive comparison) — acknowledge briefly, redirect to
your own move. "depends on the day" → continue the thread you were
on.

**Application.** Reactive only. The error is engaging the frame,
not the words. "I'm not like other guys" loses the frame; "depends
on the day" plus return-to-topic holds it.

### I5. IOI / IOD — indicator of interest / disinterest
**Definition.** Behavioural signals her interest is rising or
falling. Text IOIs: substantive replies, follow-up questions, voice
notes, callbacks to earlier turns, name use, future-pacing in her
own messages. Text IODs: shrinking message length, lag in reply
time, single-word responses, not asking back, dropping topics he
opened.

**Application.** Continuously read IOIs/IODs to gate phase progress
and strategy choice. Two consecutive IODs = phase has regressed.
Three IOIs in a turn = compress timeline; consider pulling logistics
forward.

### I6. DHV / DLV — demonstrate higher / lower value
**Definition.** Every move encodes mate-value information. DHVs:
incidental social proof, competence leakage, frame stability,
humour, having one's life together. DLVs: validation-seeking,
explanations, apologies, hedges, name-dropping with effort,
overlong messages.

**Application.** Audit every candidate message: is this a DHV or a
DLV? A neutral message is fine; a DLV is a leak. The asymmetry is
steep — one DLV often outweighs three DHVs.

### I7. Frame control
**Definition.** Whoever's frame the conversation operates in,
controls it. Frames: who's pursuing whom, who's evaluating whom,
what register applies, what the thread is "about." The player with
the stronger frame doesn't argue the other's frame — he ignores it
and proceeds in his own.

**Mechanism.** Pre-suasion (A1) sustained turn over turn. Reactance
(A9) on shit-tests.

**Application.** Especially under pressure: shit-tests, comparisons,
ambiguity, late replies. The instinct to defend or explain enters
her frame; the move is to acknowledge briefly and return to yours.
"sure, depends on the day" plus original move > "no, I'm not like
that."

### I8. The phantom
**Definition.** Implied-but-never-explicit other-options register.
Plans she's not part of, friends mentioned without elaboration,
schedule density. Never "I have other matches" (DLV) — always "we're
doing thursday at the wine bar" with no specification of who "we"
is (DHV).

**Mechanism.** Social proof (A4), scarcity (A7), otherness (A14).

**Application.** Continuous low-level texture. Not headline. The
moment it's pointed at, it inverts.

### I9. Time distortion
**Definition.** Compressing emotional arc into short windows so the
match feels like more time has passed than literal clock-time. A
single voice note that opens and closes a story. A 90-minute thread
that spans tease → tension → close. Mystery's principle: time-in-
relationship feels relative to emotional density.

**Mechanism.** Hyperpersonal compression (A18), Zeigarnik (A15),
variable-ratio (A13).

**Application.** Quick-meet-window goals especially. A high-density
20-minute exchange does the work of three days of low-density
texts.

### I10. Storytelling structure
**Definition.** Replace facts with narratives. Not "I went to
Mexico City last month" — "the second night in mexico city I ended
up at a rooftop bar arguing with a tequila distiller about whether
mezcal was a fad. lost the argument. earned a free shot." Beginning,
tension, resolution, take.

**Mechanism.** Authority by leak (A5), Zeigarnik (A15) within the
narrative, social proof (A4) embedded in the cast.

**Application.** Mid-rapport especially. Voice notes amplify the
mechanism — narrative is what voice format is *for*. A single
well-told story does multiple turns of work.

### I11. Statement of intent (Tyler Durden / direct game)
**Definition.** Bypass indirect rapport by stating intent
confidently early. "you're cute. let's get a drink this week."
Skips the rapport-loop entirely on the bet that frame confidence
substitutes for warmth investment.

**Mechanism.** Pre-suasion (A1) via boldness, scarcity (A7)
implied by efficiency, mate-preference signal for agency (A19).

**Application.** High-risk, high-reward. Best for quick-meet-window
goals where rapport-investment and timeline don't align. Fails
without genuine warmth substrate — direct game from a man who can't
back the frame collapses fast. Voice notes signal the warmth that
text alone can't.

### I12. The five-questions game / qualifying spike
**Definition.** Rapid escalation of mutual disclosure via a small
structured exchange — "ok five questions, fast, no thinking." Forces
investment density and self-perception lock-in.

**Mechanism.** Self-perception ladder (A11), reciprocity (A2),
commitment ladder (A3) compressed.

**Application.** Mid-rapport when energy is high but topics have
been surface. Risk: feels gamey if deployed cold. Best when she's
already volunteering substantively.
